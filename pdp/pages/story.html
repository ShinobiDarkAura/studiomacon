About Maçon

We Discover
by Holding

In French, maçon means “mason”: one who works with their hands to build things. Every piece here is shaped that way, and meant to be held that way.

Carried, turned over, kept close, it changes with the hand that keeps it. The patina deepens where a thumb rests and brightens where it rubs, until the piece carries the marks of a life alongside its own.

Our Practice

Carved by Hand,
Cast in Metal

Each piece begins as a sketch on paper. Next, the object is carved by hand, first in wax, then fine-tuned in metal. We work closely with a local foundry to cast the wax carvings in bronze, silver or gold, and each piece is then refined and polished in our studio.

If its path is to become a collection piece, a rubber mold is made so that we can make the children of that original and share them with you. If it's a custom piece, it is one of a kind.

Our History

Drawn
Together

Alex and Hannah met in a figure drawing class at RISD in 2008: two freshmen at neighbouring easels, learning to look at the same thing and see it differently. They became close friends, and then, as people do, drifted apart across distance and years.

When they found their way back, they married in 2023. Through all of it they made things for each other, small objects shaped by hand and given without occasion, because making was the truest way either of them knew to say it.

That is where Maçon comes from. Artifacts of love: pieces drawn from the ancient objects that have always moved them, and pieces that are themselves what remains of a love, its evidence, meant to be held.
