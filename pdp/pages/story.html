About Maçon

We Discover
by Holding

In French, maçon means mason: one who works with their hands to make things. Every one of our pieces travels through many hands before it finds the ones it was made for.

Our Practice

Carved by Hand,
Cast in Metal

Each piece begins as a sketch on paper. Next, the object is carved by hand, first in wax, then fine-tuned in metal. We work closely with a local foundry to cast the wax carvings in bronze, silver or gold, and each piece is then refined and polished in our studio.

Our Story

Drawn
Together

Alex Cohen and Hannah Woodard met in a figure drawing class at RISD in 2008. Two eager freshmen at neighboring easels, learning to make art together. She had grown up in a sylvan corner of rural Vermont, building fairy worlds from branches, leaves and stones, and high heels from construction paper and rolls of toilet paper. He had been drawing wherever he happened to be: at the table, in the car, at restaurants, in the margins of his school notes. They became close, but the journey was winding and led them apart for a decade.

When they finally found their way back to each other, they married in 2023. Through it all, they never stopped making things for one another. Little objects that kept them connected over many years and miles apart.

That is how Maçon was born. Out of the art they made for each other to express a feeling too vast for language.
Artifacts of love.
Made in Santa Monica, California.
